Twelve stages from the well to the castle gate

For every stage: when it was reached, what problem turned up, and how it was solved.

1
The Well · 11/21
  • ReachedNov 21 · From the title menu to the top of the well with no human input: 114 s, nine rounds of wall kicks.
  • ProblemThe area-name banner also shows inside the pit, so the script reported “out” while still in it and a posted video had to be withdrawn. Changing several things at once and testing two or three times each: about 1 success in 20.
  • Fix“Out of the pit” now means open sky in the frame. Fix a baseline, change one thing, record six runs before judging: 3/6 → 5/6.
2
Reservoir Stealth · 11/5 — 11/8
  • ReachedNov 6 · Past the guards and into the crawl space under the floor.
  • ProblemRetrying in batches gave anything from 0/6 to 4/6 with no visible cause; once a guard noticed the Wolf, the run was over.
  • FixNo neural network: follow the route by gradient features of reference frames. Read the alert marker over an enemy's head, white → yellow → red; it shows 4–7 s before the attack, time enough to hide under the house. Analyse one failure frame by frame instead of retrying in batches.
3
Cliff and Broken Bridge · 11/9
  • ReachedNight of Nov 9 · Along the cliff, across the broken bridge, into Kuro's room: the sword Kusabimaru.
  • ProblemGuessing the geometry wasted about 15 attempts; five ways of jumping the broken bridge all fell.
  • FixOne line from the project lead, “just push D”: hang on the ledge and shimmy. Cutting the reference video into 0.1-s frames showed that the grab key goes 0.3 s after the jump.
4
Shigenori Yamauchi · 11/9 — 11/10 · kill
  • ReachedNov 10 · First kill of Shigenori Yamauchi, the first named general. On the later new-game run he fell at the first try.
  • ProblemHolding guard only blocks; two foot soldiers killed the Wolf in 20–30 s. About 25 attempts in one afternoon, no win.
  • FixReading up showed that a deflect needs a fresh press of guard. Ignore the soldiers, run straight at the general, keep moving in the fight.
5
Temple and Prosthetic · 11/11
  • ReachedNov 11 · Through the scripted loss to Genichiro to the Dilapidated Temple: the Shinobi Prosthetic.
  • ProblemReplaying recorded keys is open loop: one pass in three. After a fight the camera pointed down and the blind replay was 622 px off; two falls. The steps printed “OK” with nothing behind it.
  • FixBefore a fight, save a frame of the way forward; afterwards turn until it matches. Every “OK” needs positive evidence on screen.
6
General Kawarada · 11/11 — 11/12 · kill
  • ReachedNov 12, 17:16 · General Naomori Kawarada killed.
  • ProblemLanding on the gatehouse roof, the Wolf was seen 3 times in 4; the red perilous-attack sign was mistaken for the deathblow dot; head-on, four fights in five were lost.
  • FixLearn the grapple marker: struck through means unusable, solid means go. Crouch 20 s, then drop for a plunging deathblow; head-on, hold guard and cut every 0.3 s.

Detour to the Stairway · 11/18 — 11/20

Gyoubu Masataka Oniwa Nov 24 — Nov 27

Three days, forty-six script versions, more than 250 fights. No single change won it; each problem solved filled one more cell of his posture bar.

  1. 1Held guard breaks after three or four blows→Firecrackers and the grappling hook, not guard alone
  2. 2Believed locked on; it was not→Detect the small white lock-on dot
  3. 3Wrong readings: posture 1.5× too high, a sliver of health read as zero→Calibrate each one against recordings
  4. 4Tuning on small batches was a random walk→Interleaved A/B series, about 250 fights
  5. 579 % of the hits landed while the guard key was up→Log every key press; press the hook until its accept flash is seen
  6. 6No wind-up to react to for a deflect→Re-press guard every 0.5 s: inside the window 57–85 % of contacts raise his posture
  7. 7Posture broken but not punished; the execution missed one click→Cut first when his bar is full; keep pressing through the execution
  8. 8Of 43 gourds only 16 were actually drunk→Test every key at an idol; three taps, confirmed by the gourd count on screen
  9. 9Firecracker thrown 15 times in 35→Wait out the block recoil; verify by the emblem count
  10. 10Phase 2: the perilous charge drags the Wolf for 0.7 of a health bar→Spot the flickering sign; two jump timings by distance
  11. 11Arriving at the fight already hurt→Fight only from full health: first kill, Nov 27, 00:43
  12. 12His answer comes 3.6–3.8 s after every hook→Split the string around it; drink on landing: four more kills the same day

Six things learned on the way Quoted lines are the project lead's, translated from Chinese

  • Analyse before retrying

    “Stop retrying at random. Analyse more.”

    Find the cause frame by frame; change one thing at a time.

  • Use the cheap method

    “Too expensive. Use traditional methods.”

    Classical image processing and scripts throughout; not one neural network.

  • Close the loop

    “Open loop always fails. It basically never gets through.”

    Landmark → action → check; events you can see instead of timers.

  • Keep your own anchors

    “Before a fight, mark your direction: save a frame.”

    Frames the script saved itself match far better than someone else's video.

  • See the action happen

    Read the gourd count. Read the emblem count.

    Ten minutes of key tests at an idol solved what three days of statistics had not.

  • Attack more

    “Attack more. Stop thinking only of defence; attacking is what kills the enemy.”

    The lower his health, the faster his posture fills.

What we learned

Sekiro gives no game internals to read, only the screen and the keyboard and mouse. In three weeks the scripts the LLM wrote went from the well of a new game to the gate of Ashina Castle and beat Gyoubu five times. These are the lessons specific to Sekiro; the cross-game ones are on the overview.

Method: how the Gyoubu fixes were found

  • Small-batch tuning is a dice rollTuning on a few fights at a time went back and forth; alternating two setups over about 250 fights showed which changes really helped.
  • Ten minutes of key tests beat three days of statisticsOnly 16 of 43 gourd drinks actually happened, and three days of statistics did not explain it; ten minutes of testing each key at an idol did: three taps, confirmed by the gourd count on screen.
  • Admit a dead endThe cliff skip around Gyoubu failed about 75 times out of 75; this version of the game does not allow it. Giving up the skip and fighting head-on was faster.

Perception: evidence on the screen

  • Calibrate every reading against recordingsPosture read 1.5 times too high and a sliver of health read as zero, so the script thought it had died. Each reading counts only after it is checked against recordings.
  • Events you can see instead of timersAgainst the Chained Ogre the dust burst of his chain breaking replaced a fixed wait; in stealth the alert mark over an enemy's head (white → yellow → red) gives 4–7 s of warning before an attack.
  • Your own saved frames match bestBefore a fight, save a frame of the way forward and turn back to it afterwards; frames the script saved itself match far better than someone else's video. The project lead: “Before a fight, mark your direction: save a frame.”

Combat: attacking wins

  • Guarding alone breaksHolding guard only blocks and breaks after three or four blows; a deflect needs a fresh press. Re-pressing every 0.5 s raises his posture on 57–85 % of contacts inside the window.
  • The lower his health, the faster his posture fillsAbove 0.9 health his posture gains only 0.14 a minute net; at 0.5–0.7 it gains 0.47, so cut his health first. The project lead: “Attack more. Stop thinking only of defence.” The attacking setup built on this won in 220 s without a death.
