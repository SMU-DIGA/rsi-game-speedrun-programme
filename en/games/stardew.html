What we learned

From floor 0 to floor 120 of the mines took about 800 in-game days and 10 real days, and the bot hit 31 pitfalls on the way. These are the lessons from them; most hold for any bot that plays a game from the screen, not just Stardew Valley.

The rule: the screen only

  • Use the screen and public knowledge onlyThe test for any piece of information: could a player who only sees the screen and may read the wiki and guides know it? The LLM once unpacked maps and villager schedules from the game's files to plan routes; the project lead called it cheating, and all of it was deleted in favour of route maps stitched from recordings and public guides.

Perception: the picture changes

  • Maps made from appearance expireA route map of the town recorded in spring matched at only 0.28 in summer, and rain and night broke the same stretch every time. Record each season and the rain separately, or keep several images of one place.
  • Recognise a person by several colours at onceLooking for one purple gave 13 false hits in 16 frames (shop shelves, the bot's own clothes); requiring three hair colours together gave none in 19 frames. A person's back looks different, so test front, back and side.
  • Accept only plausible readingsThe floor number 5 was once read as 58; the bot thought it had gone down two floors, retreated the wrong way and fainted in the mine at 2 am. Now only “previous floor + 1” is accepted, and every new background colour of the floor box gets its own template.

Control: see every action work

  • The smallest step must be smaller than the arrival toleranceOne tap moved about 52 px while “arrived” meant within 18 px, so the bot jittered beside its bed for 60 s and missed bedtime night after night. One log line per step showed it at once; a 30-px tolerance fixed it.
  • If nothing happens, change position or input instead of repeatingA door opens only from the tile in front of it, clicking that tile; at a festival right-click does nothing and the action key, facing the person, works.
  • Something that will not open may just be blockedThe shipping bin was hidden by a grown pine and ignored every click; chopping the tree and stepping closer opened it.
  • Check before anything irreversibleWhat looked like a topaz was sap, a gift she hates: friendship −40. Hover and read the name before giving, selling or trashing.

Pacing: the game's clock and calendar

  • Budget by the game clock, not wall timeNot in bed by 2 am means fainting and losing the next day too; a helper that started looking for the bed at 1:20 cost two days. Read the clock before any “go to bed”.
  • The calendar moves targetsOn festival days every shop is locked and everyone is at the beach; the shop closes on Wednesdays; cutscenes start at the door. Check the calendar before a visit and look for the skip button first.
  • After landing on a new floor, do nothing slowSorting the pack for 14 s on landing at floor 116 among monsters got the bot knocked out two floors later and its weapon lost. Landing to the next staircase now takes 1 s instead of 17.
  • Walk it once on record, then hand it to the programA new place is first walked step by step by the LLM and recorded; the recording becomes the route map and the evidence when something breaks. Only then is it written into the program.

Long runs: treat the game as always running

  • When stuck, log before you guessThe bed problem was guessed at for days; one line per step with position and key showed the cause immediately.
  • Stop for a check at key momentsBefore each dash, on any odd floor number and before a retreat the program stops and the LLM checks the screenshot before it goes on.
  • One game action per command; pause while thinkingThree times a walking script chained after a screenshot command sent the character wandering. In a real-time game the clock runs while the LLM reads code or screenshots, so every script ends with the game paused.
  • Mind memory and the backpackThe game grows to 1.9 GB; reloading after each night's save brings it to 0.95 GB, and heavy jobs run one at a time. A full pack makes things fail silently (no pickups, a cutscene reward that cannot be taken blocks the menu), so keep two slots free.
